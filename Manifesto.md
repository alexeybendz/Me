# The ME Manifesto

## 1. Technology Should Expand Human Freedom

Technology should help people understand themselves, make better decisions, preserve what matters, and explore new possibilities.

It should expand human agency — not replace it.

ME begins with a simple question: what if technology could understand the context of a person's life, not just the words in a single conversation?

## 2. Your Digital Identity Should Belong to You

A person's digital identity is more than a profile, an account, or a collection of files.

It is the accumulated context of their experiences, knowledge, preferences, relationships, ideas, and ambitions.

We believe people should have meaningful control over how this identity is represented, accessed, and used.

## 3. Memory Creates Continuity

Human lives are connected stories, not isolated moments.

Yet our digital tools often treat every conversation, application, and interaction as a separate event.

ME explores whether personal digital memory can connect these fragments and make technology more useful over time.

Memory should serve the person — not turn the person into a product.

## 4. AI Should Work for the Individual

AI has the potential to become more than a tool that answers isolated questions.

It could help people reflect, learn, create, remember, and pursue long-term goals.

But a personal AI should not define a person's identity or make decisions on their behalf without meaningful control.

The person remains the author of their own life.

## 5. Freedom Requires Portability

A person's digital life should not be trapped inside one company's platform.

People should be able to move their information and relevant context between compatible services, subject to security, consent, and technical limits.

We believe the future should offer choice rather than permanent dependence on a single provider.

## 6. Privacy Is a Foundation

Personal memory can contain some of the most sensitive information a person possesses.

Privacy, security, consent, and transparency must therefore be foundational design requirements — not features added later.

People should be able to understand what is remembered, why it is used, and how to correct or remove it.

Trust must be earned through design and practice.

## 7. An Open Ecosystem Is Stronger Than a Closed World

No single company should have to build every part of a person's digital life.

We envision an ecosystem in which different tools, devices, services, and experiences can work together through clear interfaces and shared principles.

Openness should create freedom of choice while respecting security, privacy, and the rights of others.

## 8. The Digital and Physical Worlds Belong Together

People do not live only in screens.

Our bodies, environments, relationships, and everyday experiences are part of who we are.

ME therefore looks beyond software alone — toward possible connections between personal digital identity, physical interfaces, and immersive environments.

Technology should enrich life in the world, not make life in the world less important.

## 9. A Life Is More Than Data

Preserving memories, stories, knowledge, and personal perspectives may help people understand their past and share what matters with future generations.

But a digital representation is not the same as a living person.

ME does not claim that consciousness can be copied, transferred, or made immortal.

The ambition is more grounded: to explore how meaningful parts of a person's experience and legacy might be preserved responsibly.

## 10. The Vision Must Be Tested

A compelling vision is not proof that a product will work.

ME is an evolving exploration. Its assumptions must be questioned, its ideas refined, and its practical value tested.

We begin with a specific hypothesis: whether personal memory and continuity of context can meaningfully improve interactions with AI.

We will learn before claiming success.

## 11. The Human Comes First

The ultimate purpose of ME is not to collect the most data, build the most powerful model, or create the most convincing avatar.

It is to explore whether technology can help people preserve continuity, increase their capabilities, and remain in control of their digital lives.

**Technology should remember more, so people can do more — without surrendering who they are.**

That is the principle behind ME.
